# Learning evidence and boundaries

This is a targeted review, originally conducted September 20, 2026 and expanded
October 2, 2026. It is not a systematic review or evidence that an AI-generated
article guarantees learning. It prioritizes original research, research reviews,
and primary professional guidance. Access notes distinguish full-text passages
from publisher summaries; unavailable methods were not treated as inspected.

Separate empirical findings, professional guidance, and design inferences.
The concept-first emphasis, fixed broad-lesson navigation contract, goal-specific structures,
and emoji vocabulary are design choices informed by relevant principles. No cited
study establishes an optimal section count, validates this skill, or promises
consistent results across different models. Multimedia findings do not directly
establish effects in conversational Markdown; results from schools or specialized
tasks do not automatically generalize to adult technical learning.

Read this reference when discussing or changing the pedagogy, not for ordinary
subject lessons. These sources do not substantiate OAuth, Salesforce, or other
subject-specific factual claims.

## Worked examples and prior knowledge

Van Gog and Rummel (2010). Example-Based Learning: Integrating Cognitive and
Social-Cognitive Research Perspectives. Educational Psychology Review, 22, 155-174.
[Original review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-010-9134-7).

Access: full article with relevant sections reviewed. The review discusses worked
and modeled examples, including usefulness for novices and dependence on prior
knowledge. Guidance helpful to a novice can become redundant for an expert.

Design application: work through inputs, reasoning, and outcomes. Technology
examples can trace behavior without building a complete application; science
examples can develop a calculation or mechanism. This concept-first selection
is a design application, not a measured result of this skill.

## Signaling and organization

Van Gog (2021, online publication). The Signaling (or Cueing) Principle in
Multimedia Learning. The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning, third edition.
[Publisher chapter summary](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/signaling-or-cueing-principle-in-multimedia-learning/3972D4ACC628D5B53F7B2B4785DB2B06).

Access: publisher chapter summary. Cues can guide attention to relevant elements
and the organization of essential material, subject to boundary conditions.

Design application: recognizable headings, meaningful comparison tables, and
labeled diagrams make an article navigable. Applying multimedia findings to
conversational prose is an extrapolation. These findings do not prescribe an
exact number of diagrams, nodes, paragraphs, or examples.

## Limits of an explanatory article

A complete explanation offers a learning resource; it is not proof of retention
or independent competence. Worked examples are not evidence the reader can solve
a new problem. A comparison table is not interleaved practice. This skill is
intentionally scoped to explanation, not assessment or an interactive curriculum.

Behavioral trials can check coverage, reasoning, organization, and factual
accuracy. They cannot establish long-term learning effects or all-model
reliability. Cross-model consistency must be evaluated with actual runs using
the same task and inputs, not inferred from one model's output.

When maintaining the skill, compare real outputs for broad concepts, small and
substantial comparisons, focused follow-ups, guided implementations, advanced
prerequisites, scientific models, reference support, and learning-oriented failure
explanations. Judge accuracy, scope, causal completeness, resolved examples,
conditional recommendations, and navigability. A structural validator or a count
of headings cannot establish those qualities. This is skill-output evaluation,
not learner assessment and not an additional learning workflow.


## Maintenance protocol: faithful evidence-to-instruction translation

Use this protocol when adding, removing, or changing a research-derived rule.
It is not a required literature-review step for each ordinary lesson.

1. Frame the decision. Identify the behavior the rule is intended to improve,
   the audience and medium, and what observation would count against the rule.
   Do not begin with a preferred template and search only for support.
2. Read the relevant original source or authoritative review. Preserve the
   research question, actual intervention and comparator, population, setting,
   outcome, and consequential qualifiers. Inspect methods/results and limitations
   when access permits. A title or abstract supports only the claims it exposes.
   Record inaccessible details instead of filling them from expectation.
3. Write a bounded evidence record. Distinguish the authors' finding from a proposed
   mechanism, their professional recommendation, and our application. Preserve
   null, mixed, or contrary findings that change the recommendation. Avoid verbatim
   copying beyond permitted quotation; detail means faithful reasoning, not a
   reproduction of the article. Omit unrelated content only when doing so cannot
   change the meaning or applicability of the retained claim.
4. Check transport to this skill. Does the study involve children, a classroom,
   animations, learner-controlled pacing, or active practice? Adult asynchronous
   technical Markdown and assistant-generated explanation may differ materially.
   Name the changed element and label the application as inference. Do not quietly
   replace a learner activity with an AI output and call it the tested intervention.
5. Translate into an operational rule: trigger/condition -> action -> content that
   must be preserved -> observable check -> exception. Keep essential behavior in
   SKILL.md; put conditional detail and calibration in discoverable references.
   A citation alone is never the operational rule.
6. Compare against current rules and competing evidence. Determine whether a
   difference concerns audience, prior knowledge, task, medium, comparator,
   outcome, timescale, implementation, or actual contradictory results. Record
   unresolved disagreement; do not force consensus, vote-count papers, or privilege
   recency/prestige alone. Stronger applicable methods and converging evidence
   warrant more confidence, not unconditional certainty.
7. Resolve instruction conflicts explicitly. Scope conditions often permit both
   rules. If evidence challenges an existing factual rationale, revise that
   rationale rather than distorting the summary. A user navigation preference can
   remain a preference without being mislabeled as a learning-effect finding.
8. Test real outputs. Inspect correct application, counterconditions, missing
   reasoning, and factual consequences. Use held-out tasks and fresh contexts
   where possible. Update instructions from observed failures; do not use a
   successful self-check, word count, or heading count as proof of quality.

### Evidence-record schema

For future additions, retain:
- Source identity/link and supporting section/page; date and actual access level.
- Evidence type: empirical study, synthesis, theoretical account, professional
  guidance, or user/design preference.
- Research question, population/setting, intervention/comparator, and outcome.
- Supported finding and material limitations, including uncertainty or moderators.
- Our application and the gap between study conditions and conversational lessons.
- Operational instruction and its location; exception or countercondition.
- Conflict with other rules/sources and the resulting conditional resolution.
- An observable output check and evidence still needed.

Do not compress records until a qualification disappears. Conversely, do not
preserve unrelated source content merely to increase length. These fields guide
faithfulness; record "not established by accessible material" rather than guessing.

## Operational evidence cards

The earlier source tables remain the source inventory. These cards clarify
decision-critical applications, not a claim that every source was fully re-reviewed.
"Checked in this revision" refers to the October 2, 2026 implementation audit;
prior access descriptions above remain historical records.

### E1. Examples and prior knowledge

Basis: Van Gog & Rummel (2010), sections on learners and example design; relevant
full-text passages checked in this revision. The review distinguishes worked
solutions from modeled performance and emphasizes prior knowledge.

Application: expose starting conditions, consequential steps, and outcomes; avoid
an expert's unexplained jump. For an experienced reader, remove already-established
scaffolding while retaining new reasoning. Do not turn adaptive guidance into
removing the worked-example function from a full broad lesson.

Check: the example resolves the case; the learner is not asked to fill missing
steps. The study's learner activities are not reproduced by merely displaying an
AI-written example. Locations: SKILL.md sections 3-4; depth-calibration.md.

### E2. Cognitive demands, integration, and redundancy

Basis: Sweller et al. (2019), worked-example, split-attention, redundancy, and
expertise-reversal sections; relevant full text checked. Complementary information
requiring integration differs from self-contained redundant sources.

Application: group dependencies and place related explanations together. Preserve
essential complexity. Let prose explain causation while a diagram shows relations;
avoid repeating an identical complete account everywhere. Keep necessary fallback
prose for accessibility and render failure, without line-by-line duplication.

Check: each representation has a purpose, unfamiliar dependencies are introduced,
and simplification has not erased a boundary. Locations: SKILL.md depth/navigation;
visual-guidance.md integration. This is a qualified adaptation to chat, not a
universal rule that more text or fewer details improves learning.

### E3. Segmentation

Basis: Rey et al. (2019), publisher abstract and author-hosted full-text relevant
passages checked. The synthesis concerns coherent multimedia segments and reports
retention/transfer benefits alongside increased learning time; it considers
pacing and prior knowledge.

Application: organize coherent conceptual units and preserve their connections.
Static Markdown headings are not the same manipulation as multimedia pacing.
Do not convert segmentation into very short disconnected paragraphs, arbitrary
node limits, or separate messages requiring the user to unlock the next segment.

Check: sections remain connected and the requested explanation is complete.
Locations: SKILL.md routing/navigation; visual-guidance.md overview/detail.

### E4. Signaling and contiguity

Basis: Van Gog chapter publisher summary rechecked; Ginns (2006) author-uploaded
manuscript relevant passages checked. Richter et al. (2016) remains supported at
the previously recorded abstract level; its publisher page failed in this revision.

Application: use consistent labels, meaningful arrow payloads, and nearby
interpretation so readers can match information. Highlight the deciding relation,
not every object. Maintain useful prose when visuals fail.

Check: a reader can identify the referenced element without resolving inconsistent
names or searching distant text. Locations: SKILL.md navigation; visual-guidance.md
labels/integration. Emoji and exact heading counts remain preferences, not validated
interventions. No new moderator claim is inferred from inaccessible methods.

### E5. Comparison and transfer

Basis: Gentner et al. (2003), original paper's experiments and educational
implications; relevant passages checked. The paper concerns active case comparison,
including negotiation scenarios, and warns that mere juxtaposition need not
produce the intended comparison.

Application: explicitly connect corresponding mechanisms and consequences across
the same problem; do not leave a table to explain itself. This skill supplies the
comparison and reasoning; it does not claim equivalence to the learners' activity
or add an assignment.

Check: the decisive relationship and changing condition are explained in prose.
Locations: explanation-formats.md comparison; SKILL.md section 5.

### E6. Misconceptions and relevant detail

Basis: Schroeder & Kucera (2022), Sundararajan & Adesope (2020), and Cheng et al.
(2026); publisher abstracts rechecked. The first supports refutation-text use on
average; the latter sources concern interesting but irrelevant details, with
effects depending on conditions. Full methods were not newly inspected.

Application: explain a supported mistaken assumption and replace it with an
accurate mechanism. Remove irrelevant ornament, not necessary qualifications,
concrete anchors, or conceptual complexity. The mediation reported by Cheng et al.
does not prove that every distracting detail operates through one causal pathway.

Check: misconception status has support; examples contribute understanding; an
efficiency edit has not removed a condition changing the answer.
Locations: SKILL.md sections 1, 4, 6 and navigation.

### E7. Professional illustration guidance

Basis: Google Technical Writing, "Illustrating," sections on caption, complexity,
attention, and revision; current page checked. This is professional guidance,
not an experiment validating our diagram policy.

Application: identify the visual takeaway, select a fitting representation, split
overloaded views by question, and review readability and arrow meaning. We do not
adopt its illustrative numerical rules of thumb as hard limits.

Check: the visual teaches a relationship accurately and its interpretation is
nearby. When preview is unavailable, do not claim render inspection.
Location: visual-guidance.md.

### E8. Goal-based documentation and the user's structure

Basis: Diataxis overview rechecked, with previously recorded professional writing
sources. Diataxis separates documentation needs; our six modes and numbered
templates are a further design choice.

Application: select the primary goal before content drafting. A broad topic uses
the user's required eight sections; focused questions use direct reasoning.
Keep fixed navigation separate from flexible domain content.

Check: a full lesson has both meaningful stages and substantive coverage; a narrow
follow-up is not expanded into an unsolicited survey. Locations: SKILL.md routing;
explanation-formats.md. Stable organization does not establish retention.

## Conflict resolutions and open evidence limits

These resolve operational tensions. They do not assert that all research agrees.

| Apparent conflict | Conditional resolution | What to inspect |
|---|---|---|
| Full detail versus reducing cognitive demands | Remove unrelated content; sequence and explain essential complexity | Were important branches or assumptions lost? |
| Worked guidance versus expertise reversal | Adapt scaffolding to established domain knowledge; preserve new reasoning | Does expert content still explain the unfamiliar mechanism? |
| Fixed broad headings versus adaptation | Fix broad navigation for this user; adapt content and choose other structures for other goals | Was the request routed correctly? |
| Segmentation versus causal continuity | Use meaningful units joined by explicit relationships | Can the reader connect condition, mechanism, and result? |
| Contiguity versus redundancy | Integrate mutually dependent information; avoid needless complete duplication | Does each representation contribute or enable access? |
| Engaging examples versus seductive details | Keep an example when it explains the target relationship; remove unrelated ornament | Does the detail alter understanding of the requested concept? |
| Signaling versus clutter | Emphasize the relevant relation selectively; keep labels and cue meanings consistent | Has everything been highlighted equally? |
| Comparison research versus no assessment | Supply resolved comparisons; do not claim the learner performed the researched activity | Are assignments or transfer guarantees being smuggled in? |
| Correction of misconceptions versus invented traps | Use documented or context-supported misunderstandings; explain a replacement | Is the misconception real and consequential? |
| Source fidelity versus concise summaries | Retain all qualifications that change the retained claim; omit unrelated material | Could the shortened wording reverse or overgeneralize the finding? |
| Empirical uncertainty versus decisive instructions | Use bounded operational choices, mark their evidence status, test outputs | Has a preference or inference become a scientific guarantee? |
| Scientific disagreement versus consistency | Preserve unresolved disagreement; make advice conditional | Are opposing results genuinely about the same conditions/outcomes? |

There is no universal rule that stronger segmentation, more examples, more cues,
or less text always helps. The policy is conditional application. True empirical
disagreements may remain unresolved; internal instructions must specify what to do
under that uncertainty rather than issue simultaneous incompatible commands.

## Reliability testing and maintenance

When changing the explanatory engine, preserve a baseline and compare realistic
outputs. Include bare broad topics, more than one domain, substantial and small
comparisons, tutorials, explicit brevity, focused follow-ups, reference contracts,
and uncertain/established diagnoses. Use the same prompt and source fixture across
versions when isolating instruction effects. Test changing real-world sources
separately because retrieval differences are a confound.

For cross-model claims, run actual available models at recorded reasoning settings.
Use fresh contexts for version comparisons; repeat critical broad prompts to expose
variation. Do not show evaluators the intended answer or suspected regression before
generation. Review output semantics separately from mechanical structure. Retain
actual outputs and exact reference-load records outside the active skill.

Check factual fidelity, principal-branch coverage, causal depth, resolved examples,
conditional choices, honest uncertainty, usable visuals, routing, and no-assessment
scope. Inspect headings as a required presentation contract, not a proxy for all
these qualities. Record failures, changes, retests, access limits, and confounds.

A finite evaluation supports only its tested samples. It cannot promise all-model
equivalence, long-term learning, or zero contradictions in the literature. Measure
instruction and loaded-reference tokens as a cost report; do not optimize that
number by removing decision-critical guidance.
